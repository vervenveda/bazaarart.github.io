# Art 101 — Assessment Architecture Decision and Certification Plan
Date: 2026-10-27 · Status: design specification only · Branch: main

## Non-destructive decision
The existing 36 weekly formative lessons continue to use five randomized questions from fifteen, with submitted answers locked and a four-of-five (80%) **practice** target. Preserve their scripts, identifiers, browser evidence keys, saved attempts, artwork, and links. No change to official mastery is authorized by this document.

The older `CREATIVE_ARTS_CURRICULUM_STANDARD_V1.md` describes ten weekly items from thirty. That is a proposed cross-program standard, **not the current Art 101 implementation**. The Art 101 course standard describes five from fifteen. Neither specification alone establishes exam quality. This conflict remains visible until an educator approves a versioned assessment policy; do not silently rewrite either source.

## Assessment layers
| Layer | Purpose | Proposed design | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly formative (existing) | Diagnose misconceptions and practice | 5 from 15, shuffled; 4/5 target; per-item explanations; fresh retake | No mastery or credit |
| Midterm (new, NOT implemented) | Independently test first-half understanding and studio technique | Weeks 01–18 scope; 30 reviewed objective-tagged items drawn from a larger pool, plus an original timed/controlled studio prompt and source-aware visual analysis | Educator review required |
| Final (new, NOT implemented) | Independently test course synthesis | Weeks 01–36 scope; 40 reviewed objective-tagged items drawn from a larger pool, plus unseen visual analysis, independent original studio work and portfolio defense | Educator review required |
| Studio portfolio (existing evidence; review pending) | Evaluate authentic making and revision | 100-point rubric: observation 20, technique 20, original decisions 20, evidence/attribution 15, critique/revision 15, presentation 10 | Authorized educator, not browser JS |

Midterm/final item counts are **proposals for educator review**, not completed banks, approved policy or certified exams. Questions must assess observation, materials, technique, analysis, source evaluation, ethics and transfer—not recall alone. Both exams require fresh independent prompts and alternate forms with documented equivalence.

## Exam blueprint and security boundaries
**Midterm, Weeks 01–18:** observation and line/form 25%; color/value/composition 25%; perspective/space 20%; visual interpretation and source reasoning 15%; practical technique and critique judgment 15%. Confirm coverage against actual lesson topics before item writing; this allocation is provisional.

**Final, Weeks 01–36:** foundational visual technique 20%; composition/materials 20%; print/photography/graphic communication 20%; cultural interpretation, community and curatorial ethics 20%; synthesis, critique and portfolio reasoning 20%. Confirm objectives and cognitive levels in a 36-week evidence matrix before authoring.

Use at least two independently reviewed equivalent forms, clear rubrics, original accessible visual stimuli and a documented answer key with rationale. Closed-book portions must be administered under real supervision if claimed; static HTML cannot enforce closed-book conditions, identity, attempt integrity, or confidentiality. Never embed an alleged secure final answer key in publicly delivered HTML.

## Proposed scoring and authorization
- Knowledge and studio scores should be recorded separately, each with a proposed >=80% threshold, subject to Academy approval.
- The 100-point studio rubric requires evidence of actual artwork, observations, alternatives, feedback and revisions. No automatic image-quality judgment for young children.
- A formative pass never unlocks a course or issues a certificate. Browser-local storage is not authenticated, isolated across shared learners, or synchronized.
- An official completion decision needs identity, authorization, evidence review, assessment integrity, and a tested permission/revocation workflow.
- For younger learners, use age-appropriate observation, playful exploration and caregiver/educator feedback instead of high-stakes exams; the proficiency-based Art 101 pathway must not be presented as a finished Pre-K–adult curriculum.

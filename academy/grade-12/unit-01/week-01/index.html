Week 01 · The Artist's Legacy

Unit 01 · Professional Portfolio and Exhibition Planning · One original studio assignment

Essential question

How can a curated body of work communicate an artist's sustained inquiry and growth?

Learning objectives

Define a curatorial thesis; select and sequence works using criteria; prepare professional labels and an artist statement; plan an accessible exhibition presentation.

A portfolio is a living archive of process and finished work. Students may maintain a physical, offline digital, or combined portfolio. No social account or public posting is required.

SENIOR STUDIO · GUIDED LEARNING PATH

Curating a body of work with purpose

Begin with your artistic question. Explore a model exhibition, investigate a source, make original work, and refine your presentation—one stage at a time.

Grade 12 welcomes both continuing artists and students beginning at Khaemenes Academy for the first time. Everyone receives the complete portfolio foundation—cover, catalog, artwork labels, evidence and reflection. A returning student can select from previous work; a new student can create a baseline collection and build a focused presentation over the year. No earlier grade's portfolio is required.

01Worked curatorial example · One theme, several perspectives⌄
  1. State a thesis: “Ordinary objects can reveal how a place changes through light and time.” This is a claim the selected artworks should help a viewer examine.
  2. Choose evidence: Select or create three original studies of one place or object at different times. Label medium, dimensions, date and intention.
  3. Sequence intentionally: Start with a clear observational work, follow with an experimental variation, and finish with a revised synthesis. Explain why another order might change the audience's understanding.
  4. Write interpretive labels: Describe a specific visual decision and its relationship to the thesis, not only the subject of the image.
  5. Test the experience: Invite authorized private feedback about legibility, sequence, access and whether the evidence supports the thesis; revise the presentation.

Model exhibition label: “Morning Window, graphite on paper, 2026. Narrow value contrasts describe indirect light before the room becomes bright. The restrained palette establishes the quiet beginning of this three-work sequence.”

02Research and exhibition ethics⌄

Study a documented exhibition or artist monograph from a museum, archive, or reputable publisher. Record the exhibition title, organizer or curator, date, institution, source, and one verifiable curatorial decision. Distinguish your interpretation from documented intent. Credit collaborators, seek permission for others' artwork or photographs, respect cultural context, and verify reproduction rights. Public sharing is optional and must not occur without appropriate authorization.

03Senior studio brief · A miniature exhibition⌄

Assemble a three-to-five-work exhibition from original artworks, including new baseline work if you are joining the Academy this year. Create a curatorial thesis, selection criteria, an intentional sequence, accessible artwork labels, and a 250–400 word artist statement. Include one rejected or revised arrangement and explain how critique changed your presentation. The exhibition may be physical, printed, or private digital; no social account is necessary.

04Critique, revision and professional presentation⌄
  1. Evaluate whether each selected artwork supports the thesis and contributes something distinct.
  2. Check labels for creator, title, medium, date, and dimensions where known; describe artwork accessibly in plain language.
  3. Test readability, contrast, navigation order and alternative text for any digital presentation.
  4. Record one specific curatorial revision, preserve the previous arrangement, and explain its effect.

Original lesson · Study, plan, and create

From archive to curated collection

A process portfolio preserves the journey. A presentation portfolio selects works for an audience. Neither is simply a popularity ranking; selection should communicate intention, technical range, revision, and inquiry.

Design the viewing experience

Consider order, visual rhythm, label clarity, accessibility, and whether the exhibition is physical, private digital, or printed. Avoid public sharing by default.

Write as an artist

Draft a concise statement identifying your recurring question, materials, choices, and how your work evolved. Make claims grounded in actual portfolio evidence.

Worked portfolio framework

Document each stage with a date, an artifact or observation, and a short explanation. The sequence may loop: critique can lead to new inquiry.

Week 01 · One studio assignment

  1. Inventory at least five existing artworks, or create a documented selection plan if fewer are available.
  2. Choose a curatorial theme and explain selection criteria for three candidate works.
  3. Make two exhibition sequence thumbnails and justify the final order.
  4. Design a professional portfolio cover and create labels for the selected works.
  5. Draft an artist statement and a short accessibility and presentation plan.

Submission: One completed portfolio cover or opening spread, process studies, organized evidence, and a reflection. Use affordable or reused materials where possible. The portfolio remains private unless an authorized learner or guardian chooses otherwise.

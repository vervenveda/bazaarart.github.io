KHAEMENES ACADEMY · ART 101 · UNIT 09

Week 23 · Positive and Negative Shape

Unit 09 · Printmaking and Reproducible Images · Original studio instruction

Essential question

How can artists use positive and negative shapes to develop a reproducible visual image, and how does transferring a design affect its orientation and clarity?

Art 101 is proficiency-based for teen and adult learners. Use ordinary paper and pencil; washable ink or paint is optional. Browser-local drafts are not authenticated student records.

Core principles

Figure and ground

Positive shapes are selected subjects; negative shapes are the spaces surrounding and between them. Reversing the filled region changes which shape attracts attention.

Matrix and impression

A matrix is a prepared surface from which an image is transferred. An impression is a resulting print. Raised areas commonly receive ink in relief printing.

Orientation and registration

A transferred image may reverse left to right. Registration is the deliberate alignment of a sheet or multiple color layers. Test orientation before including letters or asymmetric symbols.

Original worked demonstration · One leaf, two grounds

Draw a leaf inside a rectangle. First darken the leaf and leave the ground light; next reverse the values so the leaf becomes light. Trace a small asymmetric mark, flip the paper and observe its reversal. Create a simple two-color plan and test which narrow gaps remain legible at thumbnail size.

Worked calculation: On a 10 × 10 grid, a silhouette occupying approximately 35 squares has an estimated area of 35%. The remaining 65% is negative ground. Count partial squares consistently and describe uncertainty; this estimate is not an aesthetic grade.

Five-stage studio lesson

01 · Observe and document

Find three examples of figure/ground design in signs, packaging or nature. Sketch both the subject and the spaces between its parts. Describe edges and ambiguity before interpreting meaning.

02 · Research and attribute

Investigate two attributed printmaking examples, recording creator or community, title or description, date when known, medium and traceable source. Compare the treatment of positive and negative areas without stereotyping a cultural tradition.

03 · Experiment and construct

Make three 8 × 8 cm tests: a filled silhouette, its reversed ground, and a mirrored matrix plan. Use pencil-only simulation, paper stamps or safe foam impressions. Label materials, orientation, dimensions and pressure where relevant.

04 · Critique and revise

Develop a two-color design and test it at thumbnail size. Identify a narrow gap or detail that disappears; preserve the first draft, revise one boundary, and document the result. Do not claim all viewers respond identically.

05 · Reflect and evaluate

Explain what the transfer process changes, distinguish matrix from impression, compare estimated areas, cite sources and record limitations. Request educator review independently of the formative practice.

Independent studio packet

Submit three observational sketches, three labeled matrix trials, an original two-color print plan with a mirror-reversal check, two cited examples, a documented revision and an evidence-based reflection. Use nontoxic washable materials only; avoid sharp carving tools, solvents and heat. Pencil-only work meets the same objectives.

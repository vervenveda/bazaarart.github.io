KHAEMENES ACADEMY · ART 101 · UNIT 13 · WEEK 35

Independent Capstone Studio

Capstone, Defense and Portfolio · Original visual inquiry and studio instruction

Essential question

How can an artist synthesize research, technique and revision into an original sustained work that answers a self-chosen visual question?

This course supports teenagers and adults and includes equipment-free alternatives. Studio evidence and quiz scores are stored in this browser only; they are not authenticated grades, identity or automatic course credit.

Core vocabulary and reasoning

Capstone

A sustained culminating project integrating skills and evidence from a course of study.

Inquiry statement

A focused question guiding research, experimentation and decisions in a project.

Prototype

A deliberately limited model or study used to test an idea before final execution.

Iteration

A documented cycle of making, examining and changing a work.

Material rationale

An evidence-based explanation of why particular materials suit a creative intention.

Critique

Specific observation, analysis, interpretation and evaluation linked to useful evidence.

Studio log

Dated notes or photographs documenting choices, methods, problems and revisions.

Original worked example

A learner proposes a small mixed-media artwork responding to the question 'How can ordinary objects communicate a history of repair?' The project must combine at least three earlier Art 101 techniques and include an explicit process record.

  1. Write a specific inquiry statement: 'How can the contrast between a mended ceramic object's smooth surface and its fractured outline create a visual story of care?' Avoid treating artistic symbolism as scientific evidence.
  2. Sketch three compositions and develop a safe small prototype using graphite tonal study, print-like repetition and a collage layer. Note how each method addresses a different part of the question.
  3. Photograph the prototype, collect a peer or self-critique, then change one unsuccessful focal point while retaining the earlier version and notes.
  4. Complete a final original piece and a capstone dossier identifying two researched artistic influences, three techniques, a materials/safety ledger, revisions and limitations.

Five-stage studio investigation

01 · Observe and document

Observe: Review previous Art 101 process studies and select at least three techniques that can interact coherently. Write down a specific unresolved visual question rather than only the desire to make something beautiful.

02 · Research and attribute

Research: Examine two credited artworks or design practices linked to the inquiry. Record the primary source, maker and context, and explain how influence differs from copying.

03 · Experiment and construct

Experiment: Propose three alternatives, choose one using stated criteria, and make a prototype with safe materials or an equally rigorous drawing/digital mockup. Keep a dated decision log.

04 · Create, critique and revise

Create and revise: Produce a substantial original artwork and test the inquiry with a critique or self-review. Revise an identifiable passage, document what was lost or improved, and note how materials limit interpretation.

05 · Reflect, archive and seek review

Reflect and archive: Assemble an inquiry statement, plans, source citations, process images, prototype, final artifact, critique, 100-point self-assessment and unresolved questions for the Week 36 defense.

Independent studio assignment

Deliver a complete capstone proposal and an original art object or documented digital work using three previously studied Art 101 methods; include three preliminary designs, two researched references, a prototype, two dated revisions, safety notes, and a 500-word studio analysis.

No-special-equipment alternative

Build a paper-only capstone using graphite, patterned rubbings and cut-paper collage; document stages by sketches if photos and devices are unavailable.

Privacy, safety and rights

Do not pass off found or AI-generated images as personal handwork. Credit influences, keep permissions for outside materials and avoid embedding private names in publicly shared work.

Research and attribution

Explore these institutional starting points. Choose specific attributed works yourself and record creator, title, date if known, accession or catalog page, rights details, and what remains uncertain.

  • The Metropolitan Museum of Art · Collection. Verify creator, title, date, page and reuse rights for any specific artwork selected.
  • Library of Congress · Free to Use and Reuse. Verify creator, title, date, page and reuse rights for any specific artwork selected.

Collections and archives contain materials with varied rights. Public accessibility does not automatically establish permission to republish.

Formative practice · Five randomized questions

Five of fifteen authored items are drawn at random and answer choices are shuffled. Answer all questions before submitting; answers lock after submission. Four correct is the 80% practice target, not an Academy authorization or mastery certificate.

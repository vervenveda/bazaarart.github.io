Week 35 · Independent Capstone Studio

Unit 13 · Printmaking and Reproducible Images · Original studio instruction

Essential question

How can an artist independently develop, test, revise and defend an original body of work using the observational, technical, research and ethical practices of Art 101?

Art 101 is proficiency-based for teens and adults. This capstone may be made entirely with paper, pencil and available materials. Choose a safe, original project. Week 35 prepares the work; Week 36 presents and defends it. Browser-local formative results are not authenticated mastery.

Core vocabulary and independent practice

Proposal, inquiry and scope

A capstone proposal names a focused artistic question, audience, intended outcome, medium, materials, schedule and constraints. A feasible scope is one that can be developed, documented and revised within the studio period.

Process evidence and iteration

Process evidence includes thumbnails, material tests, drafts, decisions, dated observations and revisions. Iteration is a reasoned change based on what a test or critique revealed, not merely a new decoration.

Artist statement and defense

An artist statement connects formal choices to inquiry, sources and intended meaning. A defense identifies evidence supporting decisions, alternative approaches considered, limitations, ethical considerations and next steps. Claims should not exceed what the record shows.

Original worked demonstration · A capstone decision pathway

Fictional project: “The Shapes of Shared Space” asks how repeated forms can make a familiar room feel inviting. The artist begins with three observational sketches, tests two paper compositions, receives two readers' comments about confusing focal points, and revises contrast and spacing. The example demonstrates a process, not a required artistic style.

Worked decision record: Inquiry: can repetition guide attention? Initial evidence: three observational sketches. Alternative A uses equal-sized forms; B uses a single contrasting focal shape. Two fictional readers miss the focal point in A. The artist revises A by increasing contrast, then retests. The statement “this revision improved clarity for two readers in the simulated example” is narrower and more defensible than “everyone will understand the artwork.”

Five-stage studio lesson

01 · Propose and bound the project

Write a 200–300-word proposal with an original artistic question, intended audience, medium, materials, safety considerations, feasible scope, planned deliverables and evaluation criteria. Choose a project such as a three-piece drawing series, a mixed-media composition, an original graphic sequence or a small exhibition design. Record what success would look like without promising a particular reaction.

02 · Research and gather observations

Make at least three primary observation studies or visual investigations. Consult two attributed artistic or design references, recording creator or institution, work, date where known and traceable source. Explain influence without copying protected or restricted content. Identify uncertainties and any needed permissions.

03 · Prototype and develop

Produce at least two distinct compositional or material alternatives, documenting dimensions, techniques, constraints and decisions. Select one direction using written criteria; begin a coherent finished work or series. Preserve early versions, sketches and dated process notes.

04 · Critique, test and revise

Ask two consenting reviewers to respond to the intended focal point, clarity and craft, or use two explicitly fictional review personas if real feedback is unavailable. Record what was actually said or simulated. Make at least two purposeful revisions and explain the evidence or artistic reasoning behind each.

05 · Prepare the Week 36 defense

Organize a draft portfolio with the proposal, research, observation, alternatives, evolving final work, two revisions and a 250–400-word artist statement. Prepare three questions a reviewer might ask about formal choices, ethics and limitations. Mark unfinished work honestly; final presentation and defense occur in Week 36.

Independent studio packet

Submit a 200–300-word proposal, three observational studies, two attributed references, two alternative prototypes, dated process evidence, at least two documented revisions, a coherent developing artwork or series, and a draft 250–400-word artist statement. Include a materials/safety check and a record distinguishing actual feedback from fictional simulations.

Educator rubric · 100 points

Focused proposal and artistic inquiry (20) · Observation, research and attributed references (20) · Original prototypes and developing work (25) · Documented critique and two revisions (20) · Process evidence, ethical care and draft defense (15). This rubric supports educator review; the formative quiz does not certify mastery.

Five questions drawn from fifteen. Four correct reaches the 80% formative target. Submitted answers lock; retakes start fresh. This is not authenticated mastery.

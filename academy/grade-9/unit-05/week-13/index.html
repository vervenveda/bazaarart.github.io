Week 13 · Figure, Space and Expression

Unit 05 · Observational Drawing and Figure Studies

Essential question

How can gesture, proportion, viewpoint and spatial relationships help an artist communicate movement and expression without relying on stereotypes or unsupported interpretations?

This unit's culminating lesson connects Week 11's observation and construction with Week 12's tonal modeling. Students develop an original figure composition from observed evidence, then revise it through critique.

Core principles

Gesture and support

A line of action simplifies the movement of a pose. The relationship of ribcage, pelvis, and supporting limbs helps describe weight and balance.

Viewpoint and foreshortening

Perspective changes apparent size and overlap. A limb extending toward the viewer may appear compressed or enlarged relative to more distant forms.

Expression and context

Silhouette, rhythm, space and light can suggest action. Emotional interpretation remains contextual; avoid presenting assumptions about a subject as fact.

Worked studio demonstration · Reaching through space

Begin with a simple action line for a figure extending an arm toward the viewer. Block the ribcage as an oval, the pelvis as a tilted mass, and the limbs as overlapping cylinders. Mark the planted foot, then compare the near hand with the more distant shoulder. Use negative spaces between the arms and torso to check the pose. Add broad value families to distinguish nearer forms and the background. Compare the result to the reference and correct any mistaken overlap or support.

This is a drawing method, not a universal anatomical formula. Actual human proportions and movement vary. Work from observation and appropriate references.

A dedicated original artwork can be added here after it is uploaded to the Week 13 folder. The curriculum remains complete without a generic illustration or broken image. A useful study would compare a line-of-action sketch, simplified construction, foreshortened pose and finished tonal figure.

Five-stage studio sequence

1 · Read a pose before drawing details

Observe a consenting model, mirror pose, or appropriately credited figure reference. Identify the overall line of action, tilt of shoulders and pelvis, supporting foot or hand, and negative spaces. Make four 45-second gesture studies. Avoid guessing the person's identity or emotion.

2 · Worked example: a figure reaching forward

Imagine a standing figure reaching one hand toward the viewer. Begin with a sweeping action line, a ribcage oval, pelvis block and simple limbs. Mark the planted foot and draw the forward arm as overlapping cylinders. Because the hand is nearer, its projected size may be greater than the distant shoulder. Compare the lengths and overlaps against the observed reference rather than using a rigid universal proportion chart.

3 · Explore viewpoint and space

Draw three thumbnails of the same action from front, side and three-quarter views, using direct observation or a safe, credited pose reference. Mark the horizon or ground contact, nearest and farthest limbs, and major negative spaces. Write which viewpoint best communicates movement and why.

4 · Compose, shade and revise

Choose one viewpoint and develop a finished original figure composition with a simple setting. Apply Week 12's value-family thinking to distinguish the figure from the environment. Preserve your first draft; revise the support point, foreshortening or silhouette based on observed evidence and a viewer's feedback.

5 · Research, critique and portfolio

Compare two credited figure studies from different artists, eras or traditions using reliable museum or artist records. Record creator, title, approximate date, medium, URL and access date. Discuss differences in gesture, spatial construction and purpose without asserting universal cultural meanings. Archive your studies, alternatives, revision, feedback and a 120–200 word reflection.

Studio assignment · Evidence checklist

Label each study with the date, medium, source or observed subject, and purpose. Use consenting models or lawful, properly credited references. Preserve the first and revised versions.

Teacher/guardian rubric · 100 points

Observation, gesture and support (25) · Viewpoint and foreshortening studies (20) · Original figure composition and spatial clarity (25) · Critique and documented revision (15) · Research, reflection and portfolio evidence (15). Educator review is separate from the formative assessment.

Formative assessment · Five questions

Five questions are randomly selected from fifteen curriculum-aligned items. Four correct meets the 80% formative target. Submissions lock; retakes use a fresh selection. Browser-local practice does not independently authorize Academy progression.

Unit 05 · Final reflection

Compare your Week 11 construction study, Week 12 tonal still life, and Week 13 figure composition. In your portfolio, explain which observation habits improved, which problems remain, and how your next studio project will build on this evidence.

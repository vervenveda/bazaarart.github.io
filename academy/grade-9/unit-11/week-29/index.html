Letterform and Readability

Typography, Graphic Design and Information · Original visual inquiry and studio instruction

Essential question

How do type anatomy, spacing and contrast affect who can read and understand an artistic message?

This course supports teenagers and adults and includes equipment-free alternatives. Studio evidence and quiz scores are stored in this browser only; they are not authenticated grades, identity or automatic course credit.

Core vocabulary and reasoning

Baseline

The line upon which most letters appear to rest in a line of text.

X-height

The height of typical lowercase letters, excluding ascenders and descenders.

Counter

The enclosed or partially enclosed space inside a letter, such as the interior of o or e.

Kerning

Adjustment of space between a particular pair of letterforms.

Tracking

Adjustment of overall letter spacing across a selected run of text.

Hierarchy

Visual ordering that helps readers identify a headline, subtitle, body and supporting details.

Legibility

Ease of distinguishing individual letterforms; separate from how comfortable a long passage is to read.

Original worked example

A tiny poster announces 'ART TODAY 4 PM' using ornate script throughout. At a distance the lettering merges and the event time disappears.

  1. Sketch two display headlines using original letterforms. In version A use tight spacing; in version B open the counters and increase the spacing between 'R' and 'T'.
  2. Set the date and location in simpler letters with strong contrast. Compare at 30 cm and across a room, recording which words can be read without guessing.
  3. Place the event time directly below the main title, then reduce decorative flourishes behind critical information.
  4. Photograph the before and after, annotate baseline, counters, kerning pairs and a legibility test that includes an alternative high-contrast layout.

Five-stage studio investigation

01 · Observe and document

Observe: Collect three examples of public lettering from permitted environments. Describe which letters are hardest to distinguish and the relationship among title, date and small print.

02 · Research and attribute

Research: Study two credited design examples from a museum or archive. Record designer or creator if known, publication context, accessible text alternatives and the purpose of each typographic choice.

03 · Experiment and construct

Experiment: Draw at least eight original letters, compare thick/thin strokes, test two kerning pairs and try the same message at three sizes and two contrast levels.

04 · Create, critique and revise

Create and revise: Design a poster for a fictional exhibition; test reading distance with a volunteer or self-test and revise title, hierarchy and small-print contrast.

05 · Reflect, archive and seek review

Reflect and archive: Present the letter studies, two poster states, explicit readability observations and source citations; distinguish a decorative artistic decision from an accessibility barrier.

Independent studio assignment

Deliver an original 8-letter study, two contrasting headline experiments, a poster with clear event hierarchy, a documented reading-distance test with at least two readers or equivalent self-tests, and two annotated revisions.

No-special-equipment alternative

Use pencil, ruler and gridded paper; no fonts, tablets or design software are required. Test a larger-font printout for accessibility.

Privacy, safety and rights

Do not reproduce a typeface specimen or logo as your own original mark; credit admired designers and avoid publishing contact details for imagined events.

Research and attribution

Explore these institutional starting points. Choose specific attributed works yourself and record creator, title, date if known, accession or catalog page, rights details, and what remains uncertain.

KHAEMENES ACADEMY · GRADE 9 · UNIT 03

Week 06 · Color Relationships

Harmony · Contrast · Color blending · Artistic intention

Essential question

How can relationships between colors direct attention, organize space, and influence the feeling of an artwork?

Learning goals: distinguish analogous, complementary, and split-complementary color schemes; compare additive screen blending with physical paint mixing; design a controlled palette; justify an original composition through evidence.

This laboratory evolves the Academy's original preschool Rainbow Mixing Laboratory concept—selection, mixing, experimentation, and challenges—into a secondary-level design investigation. It does not copy the preschool game's child-facing interface or assume digital RGB blending behaves like pigments.

Three color relationships

Analogous

Hues adjacent on a traditional color wheel, such as blue, blue-green and green. Often useful for visual continuity. Vary value and saturation to maintain legibility.

Complementary

Hues across from one another on a chosen wheel, such as blue and orange in a traditional artist's model. Contrast can emphasize a focal point, but results depend on proportion and context.

Split-complementary

One base hue and the two hues on either side of its complement. This offers contrast with more options than a simple two-hue pairing.

Worked visual · palette architecture

These are designed examples, not calibrated color-wheel coordinates. Different artistic and digital color models define hue relationships differently.

Model limitation: The blend interpolates RGB channel values to simulate a digital color transition. Physical paints mix subtractively and depend on pigment chemistry, transparency, medium and illumination. Test actual paint mixtures separately; do not claim the screen blend predicts pigment results.

Week 06 studio assignment

  1. Select a still life, landscape, architectural detail, or original abstract composition. Make a small value-only thumbnail first.
  2. Use the laboratory to plan three alternative palettes: analogous, complementary, and split-complementary. Record hue, saturation, lightness, and your rationale.
  3. Choose one palette and create an original finished composition. Preserve value structure, deliberately control the dominant and accent colors, and explain the intended focal point.
  4. If materials allow, test two physical paint mixtures and compare their observed behavior with the screen blend. Otherwise, compare labeled color swatches or colored pencil layering and document the limitation.
  5. Archive the thumbnail, three palette studies, final artwork, and reflection in the portfolio begun in Week 01.

Use physical art supplies or an offline drawing tool. The laboratory supports planning; a generated palette alone is not the completed weekly artwork.

KHAEMENES ACADEMY · GRADE 9 · UNIT 03

Week 05 · Hue, Value and Saturation

Observe · Compare · Compose · Explain

Essential question

How can artists change the emotional and visual effect of a composition without changing its subject?

Learning goals. Distinguish hue, value and saturation; observe how surrounding colors affect a color's appearance; build deliberate palettes; explain color decisions using evidence from an original composition.

Three dimensions of color

Hue

The color family we name, such as red, blue or green. Changing hue can change relationships and associations, even when lightness is similar.

Value

How light or dark a color appears. Value contrast helps viewers distinguish objects, read form and identify focal points.

Saturation

The intensity or vividness of a color. Lower saturation looks more muted; higher saturation looks more vivid. Saturation is not the same as brightness.

Original worked visual

The three groups are illustrative comparisons, not calibrated color-science measurements. Your screen and surrounding colors can change what you perceive.

Worked example · One subject, three intentions

Imagine a still life with a ceramic cup, folded cloth and a piece of fruit. First sketch the same arrangement three times. In Study A, use different hues with similar values. In Study B, restrict the palette to one hue but create a clear light-to-dark structure. In Study C, combine muted surroundings with one vivid focal color. Compare where your eye goes first.

For learners who cannot reliably distinguish color, compare labeled swatches, value structure, texture, and written descriptions. The assignment does not require color vision or a digital screen.

Interactive palette laboratory

Change the three sample colors. The swatches update immediately; use them to plan a composition, not as a substitute for drawing.

Week 05 studio assignment · Three studies, one original work

  1. Choose one ordinary subject and make three small sketches with identical composition.
  2. Create Study A emphasizing hue variation, Study B emphasizing light-to-dark value, and Study C emphasizing muted versus vivid saturation.
  3. Write two observations for each study: what changed, and what remained constant?
  4. Choose the most intentional study and make one larger original artwork. Use physical media or a digital drawing tool of your choice.
  5. Archive the three studies and final work in your Week 01 portfolio catalog with title, date, medium, and attribution.

Do not submit the worked visual as your artwork. It is an example for understanding, not a tracing template.

Formative understanding check

An artist keeps the same hue but changes a color from pale to dark. Which property is primarily changing?

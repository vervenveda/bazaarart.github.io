KHAEMENES ACADEMY · ART 101 · UNIT 12

Week 33 · Community Brief and Collaborative Design

Unit 12 · Printmaking and Reproducible Images · Original studio instruction

Essential question

How can an artist turn a community's expressed needs into a useful, accessible design without confusing personal assumptions with consent or speaking on behalf of others?

Art 101 is proficiency-based for teen and adult learners. A fictional community brief and fictional stakeholder personas are valid alternatives to real interviews. Never solicit identifying information from minors or publish community statements without consent. Browser-local work is not authenticated mastery.

Core vocabulary and design reasoning

Brief, scope and constraints

A design brief defines the audience, problem, deliverables, success criteria, constraints and who may approve the result. A well-bounded scope distinguishes what the project can change from issues outside its authority or resources.

Stakeholder, participation and consent

A stakeholder may use, fund, maintain or be affected by a design. Participation can include listening, co-design and critique; it does not mean every person speaks for an entire community. Obtain permission before using names, quotations, artwork or identifying details.

Prototype, accessibility and iteration

A prototype makes an idea testable before production. Accessibility includes clear language, contrast, reading order, physical reach and alternate formats. Iteration uses documented feedback to improve the design while retaining an evidence trail of decisions and unresolved tradeoffs.

Original worked demonstration · A neighborhood art-room notice

Fictional scenario: A community art room needs a printed notice explaining its weekly open studio. The hypothetical brief requires a clear title, location placeholder, schedule, free admission, a contact method and an accessible text-only alternative. Three fictional perspectives are supplied: a new visitor needs the time immediately, a volunteer needs an easy-to-update layout, and a reader with low vision needs strong contrast and readable type. These are invented classroom personas, not actual interview results.

Worked decision record: Problem: visitors cannot locate the time. Evidence: the fictional new-visitor persona prioritizes the schedule. Revision: move “Thursday · 4–6 PM” directly below the heading and increase contrast. Verification: ask a reader to find the schedule without prompting. The design still needs a real approved location and contact before any public distribution; placeholders are intentional.

Five-stage studio lesson

01 · Listen and define

Choose a low-risk community need: an art-room notice, shared-supplies guide or exhibition welcome card. Draft a one-page brief naming audience, need, deliverables, constraints, decision authority and measurable success criteria. Use consenting adult feedback or clearly labeled fictional personas; do not invent interviews.

02 · Research and attribute

Study two attributed examples of community-centered or accessible graphic design. Record designer or institution, title or description, date if known, audience, source and any documented participation method. Separate source-reported outcomes from your interpretation.

03 · Co-design or simulate responsibly

Create two distinct paper prototypes. Include a hierarchy, concise copy, readable contrast, and a plain-text alternative. If collaborating, document each person's role and permissions; otherwise label the exercise as a simulated consultation with fictional stakeholders.

04 · Test and iterate

Give two readers three tasks, such as finding the time, identifying who may attend, and locating the contact. Record successful responses, hesitation and questions without collecting unnecessary personal details. Revise the prototype; preserve the earlier version and list at least three decisions, tradeoffs or unresolved issues.

05 · Present and defend

Deliver a brief, two prototypes, a revised design, an accessible text-only version, source notes and a decision log. Explain which feedback was real, simulated or unavailable. State who must authorize public use and what still requires verification. Seek educator review independent of formative practice.

Independent studio packet

Submit a one-page community design brief, a stakeholder and consent map, two attributed references, two original prototypes, a three-task reader test, a revised design, a plain-text alternative and a decision log with at least three documented changes or tradeoffs. A fictional scenario is sufficient; never describe fictional feedback as a real community consultation.

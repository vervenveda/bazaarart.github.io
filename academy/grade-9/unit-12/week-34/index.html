KHAEMENES ACADEMY · ART 101 · UNIT 12

Week 34 · Curating an Exhibition

Unit 12 · Printmaking and Reproducible Images · Original studio instruction

Essential question

How can a curator select, sequence, interpret and present artworks so that an exhibition communicates a defensible idea while respecting artists, audiences, access and evidence?

Art 101 is proficiency-based for teen and adult learners. This exhibition can be entirely fictional and paper-based; no venue, public upload or permission to display another person's work is assumed. Browser-local evidence is not authenticated mastery.

Core vocabulary and curatorial reasoning

Curatorial thesis and selection

A curatorial thesis is the central question or interpretation organizing an exhibition. Selection criteria identify which works advance that idea and which exclusions may distort the story. A curator should distinguish documented facts from an interpretive argument.

Sequence, adjacency and sightline

Sequence is the order in which visitors encounter work; adjacency creates comparisons between neighboring pieces. Sightlines, scale, spacing, lighting and circulation influence what is noticed. A floor plan should consider a visitor's likely route and alternative paths.

Interpretation, access and permissions

Wall labels, an introductory panel, accessible text and object records help visitors understand the work. Crediting creators and sources is necessary, but attribution alone does not grant reproduction or display rights. Identify consent, provenance and accessibility issues before public presentation.

Original worked demonstration · A six-work exhibition

Fictional exhibition: “Patterns of Everyday Life” presents six original classroom works: a charcoal cup drawing, a geometric paper collage, a photographed window study, a hand-lettered notice, a texture rubbing and a small cardboard sculpture. These are hypothetical works for layout practice, not an actual collection. Curatorial thesis: repeated forms can make familiar objects newly visible.

Worked curatorial decision: Place the charcoal cup drawing beside the geometric collage to compare repeated contours, then place the window study beside the notice to discuss how framing changes attention. Give each work a short factual label and a separate interpretive sentence. This schematic is not a certified accessible architectural plan; a real exhibition requires venue measurements, clearances and safety review.

Five-stage studio lesson

01 · Investigate exhibition strategies

Compare two attributed exhibitions or collection displays. Identify thesis, selection, sequencing, interpretive panels, credit lines and accessibility information. Distinguish what the curators state from your own reading and record the sources.

02 · Define a collection and thesis

Choose six original works, or invent six clearly labeled hypothetical works. Write a one-sentence thesis, two selection criteria and one exclusion risk. Create object records containing title, maker or attribution, date if known, medium, dimensions if known, ownership or display-permission status and source.

03 · Plan visitor experience

Draw a floor plan or linear digital sequence. Number six works and show a suggested route, at least two intentional adjacencies, an introduction, a conclusion and an alternative reading order. Identify how visitors will find large-print or plain-text interpretation.

04 · Write, test and revise

Draft a 120–180-word introductory panel and six concise labels. Ask two readers to explain the exhibition's thesis and find an artwork's maker or source. Record misreadings and revise at least one label and one placement; retain before-and-after versions.

05 · Defend and prepare handoff

Submit a curatorial statement explaining selection, order, interpretation and limitations. Include an access checklist, permissions ledger, source notes, reader feedback and unresolved questions. Do not claim a simulated floor plan meets real venue requirements. Request educator review independent of formative practice.

Independent studio packet

Submit two attributed exhibition studies, a six-work object list, a curatorial thesis and selection rationale, a labeled floor plan or sequence, a 120–180-word introduction, six work labels, an accessibility and permissions checklist, two-reader test notes, a before-and-after revision and a curatorial defense. Paper-based plans and fictional collections are fully acceptable.

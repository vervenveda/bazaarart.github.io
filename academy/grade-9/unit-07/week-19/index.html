Week 19 · Visual Communication and Composition

Unit 07 · Culminating Design Studio

Essential question

How can an artist combine balance, rhythm, hierarchy, text and imagery to communicate a purposeful message to an audience?

Build on Weeks 17 and 18 by designing, testing and revising an original composition that communicates a clear idea.

Three communication lenses

Purpose and audience

Identify who the work addresses, what they should understand and how the artwork's choices support that goal.

Hierarchy and legibility

Organize a focal point, supporting information, typography and negative space into a coherent reading order.

Rhythm and revision

Use repeated motifs and visual intervals deliberately, then evaluate actual viewer responses and improve the design.

Original worked demonstration · Community Art Day

Create a 20 × 30 cm poster for a free community exhibition. Write a short design brief and draw two alternative layouts. In one, place a large circular art motif in the upper-left, three smaller repeated circles across the composition, and the event title in a quiet high-contrast text area. In the other, center the motif and arrange the supporting text beneath it. Test both at thumbnail size. Ask a viewer which information they noticed first, and revise the weaker reading order. The final artwork must use accurate event information or clearly label fictional sample details.

Five-stage learning sequence

1 · Investigate message and audience

Choose an authentic, age-appropriate subject: a school art exhibition, a neighborhood garden, a library event or a conservation campaign. Write a brief identifying the intended audience, the one idea the viewer should remember, a desired response, and any constraints. Examine two credited examples and distinguish observable design choices from interpretations.

2 · Construct a visual hierarchy

Make three contrasting thumbnails: centered symmetry, asymmetrical focal balance, and a rhythm-led arrangement. Mark the first, second and third points of attention. Reserve negative space for essential text. Test title-to-body size relationships, value contrast and whether the image remains legible when reduced.

3 · Worked example · A community art exhibition

Brief: invite families to a free community exhibition. Primary message: 'Community Art Day'. Secondary message: the date and venue. Supporting message: a short invitation. Use one large circular artwork motif near the upper-left, repeat three smaller circles diagonally, and counterbalance the cluster with a clear text area. Draft a second layout with a centered focal point. Compare how both guide attention and which communicates the event details more clearly.

4 · Produce, test and revise

Create an original final poster or visual essay, physical or digital, incorporating a purposeful focal point, at least one repeated motif, deliberate negative space and readable text. Test it with a reviewer: ask what they noticed first, what the work is about and what action they would take. Record the answers without coaching. Revise at least two choices and preserve the before-and-after versions.

5 · Research, critique and portfolio synthesis

Research two relevant examples from credited artists, museums or design institutions. Record creator, title, date when known, medium, institution or URL and access date. Compare the role of composition and audience without assuming universal interpretation. Submit a 150–250 word reflection and a Unit 07 comparison showing what improved between Weeks 17, 18 and 19.

Teacher/guardian rubric · 100 points

Design brief and audience clarity (20) · Hierarchy, balance and visual rhythm (30) · Originality, execution and legibility (20) · Viewer test and documented revision (15) · Research, reflection and portfolio evidence (15). Educator review is separate from formative practice.

Formative assessment · Five questions

Five randomized questions from fifteen lesson-aligned items. Four correct meets the 80% practice target. Submitted answers lock; retakes begin a fresh attempt. Browser-local results do not independently authorize Academy progression.

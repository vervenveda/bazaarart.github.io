01 · Observe a worked example

Arrange a mug beside a book. Use the book's height as one reference unit. If the mug appears approximately three-quarters as tall, mark that relationship before adding details. Check the negative space between the objects. Draw once by estimation and once by measured comparison, then identify the relationship that changed most.

02 · Research and interpret art

Select a museum-documented observational drawing or architectural study. Identify one proportion or spatial relationship that can be described from the image. Verify attribution, title, date or period, medium, and source; do not assume the maker used a particular measurement method unless the source documents it.

Use an institutional catalog or credible publication; credit sources and respect image-reuse rights.

03 · Make original studio work

Make two observational arrangements using the same three objects. Create a measured drawing with at least three recorded relative dimensions and two negative-space checks. Preserve a first attempt, revised drawing, and a short explanation of the largest proportional correction.

Use physical materials or an appropriate digital medium. Original physical work should be retained even if a digital record is created.

04 · Critique and revise
  1. Describe the observable result without judging it first.
  2. Compare the result against your stated artistic intention and the demonstrated method.
  3. Choose one specific correction, revise it, and preserve the earlier version.
  4. Explain what improved, what remains uncertain, and what you will practice next.
05 · Review and document growth

Use the existing 100-point reviewer rubric and Academic Evidence record below. Check these dimensions: Comparative measurements; Negative-space reasoning; Documented correction.

Emerging: limited observational evidence. Developing: partial evidence and basic revision. Proficient: complete, accurate studies with explained revision. Advanced: multiple controlled comparisons, precise reasoning and a clear next investigation.

These descriptors are formative. The standalone page cannot certify the Academy's 80% mastery requirement.

Essential question

How can artists estimate proportion without guessing?

Proportion compares the sizes of parts within a whole. Relative measurement uses one observed dimension as a reference. Negative space is the visible space around and between forms; comparing its shapes can reveal errors in a drawing.

Observe

Arrange a mug, book, and small object. Choose one dimension as your unit and compare other heights and widths to it.

Measure

Hold a pencil at arm’s length, close one eye, and mark a reference length with your thumb. Keep your arm at the same distance.

Revise

Check at least two negative spaces between objects. Redraw one incorrect relationship without tracing the original.

Worked example · Comparing height and width

The architectural example introduces vertical and horizontal measurement. Now compare the ratio with the simplified mug study below; the same principle applies to subjects of different shapes.

For this illustrative object, the height-to-width ratio is 4:3. If your drawing is 12 cm high, a matching body width is 9 cm. Real objects vary: measure your actual subject rather than copying these example numbers.

One weekly assignment · Proportion study

  1. Make a thumbnail of a three-object still life.
  2. Record at least four relative measurements in a small table (reference, observed ratio, drawn ratio, correction).
  3. Draw the positive shapes and at least two negative-space shapes.
  4. Complete a final observational drawing with revised proportions.
  5. Archive the first attempt, final drawing, and written comparison as one Week 03 assignment.

Materials: pencil, eraser, paper, ruler or straight edge, and three safe household objects. No camera or paid materials required.

Interactive proportion laboratory

Change the example drawing height and width. Compare its ratio to the observed reference ratio of 4:3. This models measurement reasoning; it does not measure a real object for you.

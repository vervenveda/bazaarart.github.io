Week 01 · The Artist as Researcher

Unit 01 · Inquiry, Influence and Ethical Attribution · One original studio assignment

Essential question

How can an artist learn from cultural and historical sources while developing original work?

Learning objectives

Form a researchable visual question; distinguish primary observation from contextual claims; document sources; connect artistic influence to independent studio choices.

A portfolio is a living archive of process and finished work. Students may maintain a physical, offline digital, or combined portfolio. No social account or public posting is required.

Original lesson · Study, plan, and create

Investigate before imitating

Choose an artwork, object, or documented artistic tradition. Begin with visible evidence—materials, motifs, arrangement—then research its historical or cultural context using a trustworthy collection or publication.

Build an ethical research portfolio

Create separate sections for observation notes, source records, visual experiments, original compositions, and critique. Record creator, title, date when known, collection or publisher, and link or reference.

Transform influence into original expression

Study a technique or design principle without copying the composition wholesale. Make two experimental studies and explain what you changed and why.

Worked portfolio framework

Document each stage with a date, an artifact or observation, and a short explanation. The sequence may loop: critique can lead to new inquiry.

Week 01 · One studio assignment

  1. Write one focused research question about a selected artwork or tradition.
  2. Record at least two attributable sources, noting uncertainties or differing interpretations.
  3. Create an annotated observation study and an independent experiment.
  4. Design a research portfolio cover that visually expresses your inquiry without misrepresenting the source culture.
  5. File an attribution log, studies, original design, and reflection.

Submission: One completed portfolio cover or opening spread, process studies, organized evidence, and a reflection. Use affordable or reused materials where possible. The portfolio remains private unless an authorized learner or guardian chooses otherwise.
